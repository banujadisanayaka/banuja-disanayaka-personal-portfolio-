03 / THE PERSON

Different disciplines.
One curious mind.

My work moves between people, systems and visual stories. Running a busy outlet taught me to notice what gets in the way. Design taught me to make things clearer. Building products gave me a way to connect the two.

Whether I am mapping an ordering workflow or waiting for light on a quiet street, I start by paying attention.

My path has taken me from graphic design and advertising to outlet operations in Kuala Lumpur. Today, those experiences inform products that organise real work, alongside a photographic practice that keeps me looking closely.

PEOPLE / PROCESS

The operator

Understand the real work.

Working in an outlet means paying attention to people, timing and the details that hold a day together. Team coordination, stock, suppliers, production and order handling gave me a practical view of how small gaps between tasks become larger problems.

That experience feeds directly into TFC Orders Tracker: organise the handoff, make the next action clear and keep exceptions visible.

Inside the operations project ↗
FORM / MEANING

The designer

Make the message clear.

My design work spans logo design, education-related communication and advertising. It taught me to consider what someone sees first, what they need to understand next and how a visual choice supports the message.

VELORIA brings that thinking into a wider identity system, where distinct creative voices need a recognisable relationship.

Explore the identity system ↗
IDEAS / SYSTEMS

The builder

Turn a question into a tool.

Building products gives me a way to work on the problems I notice. My projects explore operational visibility, student administration, local knowledge and voice assistance. Each asks a different question about how software can support everyday work.

I use AI-assisted development as part of the process, with the product direction, source review and validation still needing deliberate human judgement.

Explore a knowledge workspace ↗
LIGHT / LIFE

The observer

Give the ordinary another look.

Photography is another way of paying attention. The Daily Gaze collects light, streets, nature, heritage and everyday details from Sri Lanka and Malaysia. It gives the quieter, more observational side of my work its own space.

The same habit connects the camera with design and product work: notice what is there before deciding what to add.
